The source

What runs off the land settles in the lake. We bring it back.

Eutrophic lakes collect decades of nitrogen, phosphorus and organic matter washed in from the surrounding watershed. That load sinks and builds into a dark, living sediment on the lake floor. Pulling it out helps the lake. Putting it into soil feeds the plant.

1Recover

Dredge the floor

Sediment is lifted from the lake bottom where nutrients have concentrated over years of runoff.

2Test

Screen every batch

Each lot is lab tested for nutrient content and screened for heavy metals and contaminants before it goes any further.

3Cure

Dry and condition

The muck is dewatered, cured and screened into a consistent, easy-to-mix product.

4Grow

Back into soil

Top dress it, mix it into your media, or brew it as a tea. The nutrients go back to work.

Why muck

What we're testing for.

Y

Yield

Lake sediment has been linked to more root growth, more biomass and higher yields in food crops. We're testing how it performs on cannabis, and we'll publish the numbers when the results are in.

R

Resin and cannabinoids

Our goal is fuller trichome coverage and natural cannabinoid and terpene expression, so the strain's true flavor comes through. Testing will show how close we get.

S

Soil life

Lake sediment carries organic matter and minerals, including calcium, that build structure and feed the microbes your roots depend on.
